Design tradeoffs for a low-cost optical neonatal bilirubin screen

A hands-on tool for results from open data (NeoJaundice — 745 neonates, forehead photos + measured serum bilirubin). The accuracy ceiling comes from how you capture the image and what you measure with — not the model architecture: skin-only colour features reach AUC 0.82, and no CNN or transfer network beats it.

Patient-independent 5-fold cross-validation · skin-only colour features AUC 0.82 / +clinical 0.84 · label-shuffle negative control 0.48. Including the in-frame colour reference card in the crop raises AUC to 0.88 — acquisition control is worth +0.05. NeoJaundice is a jaundice-evaluation cohort, not consecutive births.

This is a research demo. It is not a tool for estimating bilirubin from real infant photos. The 95% limits of agreement for individual predictions are ±5.4 mg/dL, and optical screening is a screening layer that feeds blood confirmation, not a standalone diagnosis. The Bland–Altman mean bias is zero, but a proportional bias under-estimates infants above bilirubin 15 by 2–5 mg/dL.

Limitations: the photo frame contains a colour reference card partly included by the older crop → the skin-only crop drops AUC 0.88→0.82 (a tight crop also reduces skin area, so part of the drop may be an area effect) · policy % and curves are based on the card-included model (0.878) and a skin-only recompute (0.838) is pending · single cohort · NeoJaundice is a jaundice-evaluation cohort (not consecutive births) · few-band and melanin results are RGB approximations · fixed-threshold simplification · label-shuffle negative control AUC 0.48 (no leakage). Data: NeoJaundice (CC0), patient-independent 5-fold.
